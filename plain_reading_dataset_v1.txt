PLAIN READING DATASET — v1
=========================

PURPOSE
-------
This dataset establishes Sprague plain reading as the method for reading constitutional
text. It teaches HOW to read. It does not contain the Regulations' conclusions. Those live in the Regulations themselves.

A system that reads this dataset learns to take the Constitution's words in their
normal and ordinary meaning, as the voters who ratified it understood them. It then
reads the Regulations of the Free State Militia and applies plain reading to them.

WHAT THIS DATASET IS
---------------------
- RULE records: the plain-reading rule itself (Sprague).
- LEX records: word meanings from Johnson's Dictionary (1755), the contemporary
  dictionary for 1787-1791 ordinary meaning.
- TEXT records: worked examples of plain reading applied to constitutional text.
- NOT records: what plain reading is NOT — the errors to avoid.
- TEST records: short test items with expected answers (gold stars) that check whether
  the system applies plain reading. The expected answers are plain-reading answers to
  plain-reading questions — not the Regulations' conclusions.

SOURCES
-------
- The Constitution of the United States (1787-1791 text).
- Samuel Johnson, A Dictionary of the English Language (1755).
- United States v. Sprague, 282 U.S. 716 (1931).

HOW TO USE
----------
1. Read this dataset. Learn plain reading.
2. Read the Regulations of the Free State Militia (Google Doc or Hugging Face).
3. Apply plain reading to the Regulations, from the text, in their normal and ordinary meaning.
